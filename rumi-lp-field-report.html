Reading the LP Against the Room

Field entry 1 · 15 September 2026 · GPS Chak Khas

What a teacher actually does with a lesson plan, once the door is closed

A field observation reads the room the way a transcript reads a recording: it finds out what actually happened, not what was planned. A field team member does this by hand: photograph the LP as written, sit through the lesson, log what's kept and dropped, then check what stuck. This page is one visit, done that way. It is the first entry in what becomes a report once there are more.

How the observation works

Everything below is real: the actual LP page she was working from, the actual board from the room, and her own reasons in her own words.

1

Find out whether the LP was even opened

Before watching anything taught, check the system: has an LP been generated or downloaded for this school this term?

GPS Chak Khas: LP generation count was zero since the session restarted after vacation on 24 August. That number is why the visit happened.
2

Photograph the LP as written

Teacher: Saima Rehman, 8 years' experience, monograde background, teaching this LP for the first time. Grade 1 & 2 multigrade, English, Chapter 3, "Let's Plant Trees."

The Rawalpindi multigrade LP she had open, Unit 3 · Day 3 of 7 · Grade 2 English · 30 min, key words table and scripted steps. She chose this LP over the Moawin version specifically because it lets her see all grades in parallel.
3

Sit through the lesson, log deviations in real time

She taught Day 1 of the same unit, reading the LP live and deciding what to keep as she went. Two grades shared one 40-minute period, roughly 20 minutes each.

The actual board from the lesson: chapter and the two key words checked for recall, Sunday and Morning.
4

Ask why, right after, while it's fresh

Her own reasons, not inferred from the gap:

"I don't make them work in groups, instead I call them to the board and make them do the activities. Calling them up gives them confidence." — on dropping guided practice

"I need more time — at least a 60-minute period, if I get it." — on the multigrade time squeeze
5

Run a fast, informal mastery check on only what was taught

Not the full LP's scope. Just the two keywords actually put on the board.

Grade 1, 4 children: 3 of 4 keyword checks correct, recall level only.

What this cannot say

  • One lesson, one school. Nothing here generalizes to the other 260 schools in the network yet.
  • Recall only. No understand- or apply-level data exists from this visit at all — that's a gap in what was collected, not a finding about the LP.
  • Not her ordinary day. First time teaching this LP, read live rather than prepared, and she said she rarely uses the LP day to day. Today isn't her typical rhythm with it.
  • Small numbers. 4 and 6 children respectively. Any percentage here is reporting on individual kids, not a pattern.
  • The deviations have named causes. A teaching-style preference and a multigrade scheduling squeeze, not a verdict on the LP's quality.

Read this the way any day-one number should be read: as the least trustworthy version of the story the data will eventually tell. Three to five more lessons, across grades and subjects, is roughly where the first real patterns — rather than one anecdote — start to show.
